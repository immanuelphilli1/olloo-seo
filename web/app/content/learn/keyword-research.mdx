Keyword research is finding the words people type into search engines, then picking the ones you can rank for and that help your business. Start with broad seed keywords, add modifiers to turn them into real searches, and vet each idea for business potential, intent and difficulty. Then add one new test for the AI era: can an AI Overview fully answer the query? If it can, the click may never come.

## What is keyword research?

Keyword research is the process of finding the search terms your customers use and choosing which ones to target. It has two steps. First, you generate keyword ideas. Second, you vet them to see which ones deserve a page.

Keywords are the foundation of SEO. Rank for keywords nobody searches and you get no traffic. Rank for keywords that do not fit your business and you get visitors who never buy. For the bigger picture, read [what SEO is and how it works](/learn/what-is-seo).

You need a keyword tool. The Ahrefs SEO course uses Ahrefs Keywords Explorer. Free options include the Ahrefs Keyword Generator and Google Keyword Planner. Google Search Console is free too, and shows keywords your site already gets clicks from.

## Step 1: Start with seed keywords

A seed keyword is a broad, one or two word term that describes your niche. Every keyword list starts with seeds.

Say you run a golf blog that earns affiliate commissions. Your seeds might be "golf balls", "golf clubs" and "golf hats". A specialty coffee store might use "coffee beans", "espresso" and "cold brew". A SaaS company that sells proposal software might use "proposal software" and "proposal template".

Paste your seeds into Ahrefs Keywords Explorer and open the matching terms report. In the Ahrefs course, three golf seeds produced around 125,000 keyword ideas. That is far too many to review by hand, so the next step is to narrow the list.

To brainstorm seeds fast, the Ahrefs AEO course suggests asking an AI assistant. Describe your site, how it makes money and your audience. Then ask for 10 seed keywords and five or more modifiers that do not share the same words.

## Step 2: Add modifiers to find real searches

A modifier is a word you add to a seed to make a more specific search. Modifiers also reveal the search intent before you look at Google.

| Modifier | What it signals | Example |
|---|---|---|
| best, top, review, versus | The searcher is comparing products | best golf balls |
| how, what, why, guide, tutorial | The searcher wants to learn | how to clean golf clubs |
| buy, price, near me | The searcher is ready to act | buy used golf clubs |
| calculator, checker, generator, template | The searcher needs a tool | golf handicap calculator |

In Keywords Explorer, add your modifiers in the include filter and choose "any word". Then set a minimum search volume. In the Ahrefs course, a minimum of 300 monthly searches cut the golf list to 351 ideas. Use broad seeds like "golf" with modifiers, or you will get too few results.

## Step 3: Mine your competitors' top pages

Your competitors' top pages reveal keywords your seeds will never surface. Keyword tools only return ideas that contain your seed words, and many great topics do not.

Your organic competitors are the sites that rank for the keywords you want. They are not always your business rivals. For the golf blog, the Ahrefs course names Golf Digest, golf.com and GolfWRX.

Enter a competitor's domain in Ahrefs Site Explorer and open the Top pages report. The Ahrefs course found a Golf Digest page on game improvement irons with around 5,400 monthly US search visits. A page on what degree a sand wedge is had around 4,700. Neither contains the original seeds. Note good ideas, add new seeds like "sand wedge", and repeat for each competitor.

## Search volume vs traffic potential

Traffic potential is a better guide than search volume. Search volume is how many times a keyword is searched each month. Traffic potential is how much traffic the number one page gets from all the keywords it ranks for.

Volume misleads in two ways. First, many searches end without a click. Ahrefs found that "kilometers to miles" gets around 478,000 US searches a month, but 80% end without a click because Google shows a calculator in the results.

Second, one page ranks for many keywords. In an Ahrefs study of 3 million keywords, the top-ranking page also ranked in the top 10 for nearly 1,000 other keywords on average. The Ahrefs "SEO checklist" page gets around 1,500 US visits from that one keyword, but about 3,000 visits in total.

To see traffic potential, check the estimated traffic of the top pages in the SERP overview.

## Score business potential from 0 to 3

Business potential is how much a keyword is worth to your business. Give each keyword a score from 0 to 3. The higher the score, the easier it is to recommend what you sell on that page.

Here is the scale from the Ahrefs course, for a golf site that sells used clubs:

| Score | Meaning | Golf example |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Your product is the answer | buy used golf clubs |
| 2 | Your product fits in naturally | best golf clubs |
| 1 | Hard to mention your product, but the audience is right | what is a handicap in golf |
| 0 | No link to your business | Happy Gilmore review |

Focus on 3s and 2s first. Use 1s to build topic coverage later. Skip 0s.

## Vet every keyword with B.I.D.

B.I.D. is a three-part test from the Ahrefs AEO course: business potential, intent and difficulty. A keyword should pass all three before you build a page for it. Some keywords look great on metrics and are still a trap.

### B is for business potential

Ask: if I rank number one, does it help my business? The Ahrefs course compares "what is espresso" with "best espresso machine under $500". The first has solid volume, but the searcher may never buy. The second shows buying intent and a budget.

### I is for intent

Google the keyword and see what ranks. If every top result is a store page and you planned a blog post, you will not rank. Match the intent or move on. Our guide to [search intent and the 3 C's](/learn/search-intent) shows how to read the results.

### D is for difficulty

Check how strong the top pages are. In Ahrefs, look at referring domains (how many sites link to each page) and Domain Rating (the strength of the site's links). More links and higher DR mean tougher competition. A few low-DR sites in the top 10 is usually a good sign.

Topical authority counts too. In the Ahrefs course, a DR 42 plumbing site outranks far stronger sites for "how to unclog a toilet", because the whole site is about plumbing.

## Apply the AI filter

The AI filter asks one question: can an AI Overview fully satisfy the searcher? If it can, few people will click, even if the keyword passes B.I.D.

Ahrefs data from its AEO course shows how common this is. AI Overviews appear on about 21% of all keywords, nearly 58% of question queries and 46% of queries with seven or more words. And 99.9% of keywords that trigger an AI Overview are informational.

To apply the filter, Google the keyword and read the AI Overview as the searcher would. Are you satisfied, or do you still need to click? If the AI answer is complete, the keyword may not be worth targeting for clicks. Aim instead to get your brand mentioned inside the AI answer. ## Target tool keywords AI cannot replace

Tool and calculator keywords are some of the safest targets in the AI era. When someone needs to do something, a written answer is not enough. They need a tool.

The Ahrefs AEO course found no AI Overview for "backlink checker", "mortgage calculator" or "word counter" when it checked. To find these keywords, add modifiers like calculator, checker, generator, tool, template, finder, planner and maker to your broad seeds. You can also set the intent filter to transactional.

## Put your keyword list to work

A good keyword list is short and ranked. Keep keywords with business potential of 2 or 3, an intent you can match, beatable competition and a reason to click. Start with those.

## FAQ

### How do I find keywords with low competition?

Look for keywords where a few low Domain Rating sites rank in the top 10. Also look for top pages that do not closely match the searcher's intent. Both are signs you can compete, especially if your site focuses on the topic.

### Is search volume or traffic potential more important?

Traffic potential is more reliable. Many searches end without a click, and one top page often ranks for hundreds of related keywords. Traffic potential shows the traffic the top page really gets.

### What is the B.I.D. formula in keyword research?

B.I.D. stands for business potential, intent and difficulty. It is a three-part test from the Ahrefs AEO course. A keyword should help your business, have an intent you can match and have competition you can beat.

### Should I still target keywords that show an AI Overview?

Sometimes, but treat them differently. If the AI Overview fully answers the question, aim to be mentioned in the AI answer rather than to win the click. Commercial, transactional and tool keywords still earn clicks more reliably.
