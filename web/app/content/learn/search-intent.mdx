Search intent is the reason behind a search. To match it, Google your keyword, study the top results, and copy what they have in common: the content type, the content format and the content angle. These are the 3 C's of search intent. If your page does not match them, it will struggle to rank, no matter how well it is written.

## What is search intent?

Search intent is the goal a person has when they type a query into Google. Google's job is to show the most relevant result for every query. So the pages that rank are the pages that best fit that goal.

The Ahrefs SEO course gives a clear example. The query "slow cooker recipes" shows mostly blog posts that list recipes. The query "slow cooker" shows mostly online store category pages. The topic is almost the same, but the goal is different. A product page will not rank for the first query. A recipe post will not rank for the second.

That is why intent comes first. It tells you what kind of page to build before you write a word. If you are new to how Google ranks pages, start with [our plain-English guide to SEO](/learn/what-is-seo).

## The four types of search intent

Every search falls into one of four broad types of intent. The Surfer SEO course explains them with dog training keywords:

| Intent type | What the searcher wants | Example keyword |
|---|---|---|
| Informational | To learn something | how to train your puppy |
| Commercial | To compare options before buying | best dog training classes |
| Transactional | To buy or sign up now | dog training class near me sign up |
| Navigational | To reach one specific brand | PetSmart dog training |

Informational searches suit guides and tutorials. Commercial searches suit reviews and "best" lists. Transactional searches suit product, category and sign-up pages. Navigational searches belong to the brand being searched, so they are rarely worth targeting unless that brand is you.

## How to find search intent by reading the SERP

The fastest way to find search intent is to Google the keyword and look at what ranks. SERP stands for search engine results page. The top 10 results show what Google believes searchers want. Google has already done the research for you.

When you read a SERP, check three things:

1. **The organic results.** What kind of pages are in the top 10?
2. **The titles and URLs.** If they include the keyword or a close variation, those pages are targeting it on purpose.
3. **The SERP features.** An AI Overview, "People also ask" boxes, a video carousel or a local map pack all hint at what searchers want. A map pack means the search has a local angle.

## The 3 C's of search intent

The 3 C's turn a SERP into a plan for your page. Sam Oh teaches them in the Ahrefs SEO course. They are content type, content format and content angle.

### 1. Content type

Content type is the kind of page that ranks. The main types are blog posts, videos, product pages, category pages and landing pages. For "best golf shoes", the top results are blog posts. So a product page is the wrong choice.

### 2. Content format

Content format is how the page is laid out. Common blog formats are how-to guides, step-by-step tutorials, list posts and opinion pieces. A landing page might be a tool or a calculator. For "best golf shoes", every top result is a list post. The word "best" means the searcher wants a comparison.

### 3. Content angle

Content angle is the hook, the main reason to click your result. For "best golf shoes", the top pages use a freshness angle, with the current year in the title. Other common angles are "for beginners", "cheap" or "step by step". Sam Oh calls angle the least important C and the least consistent across top pages.

## A worked example of the 3 C's

Here is how the 3 C's work for "how to swing a golf club", taken from the Ahrefs course:

- **Content type:** mostly blog posts, but a YouTube video ranks above them. Making both a post and a video could win two spots.
- **Content format:** every result is a how-to. The topic needs steps, so a step-by-step guide fits.
- **Content angle:** most pages target beginners or the basics.

Now compare "golf clubs". Every top result is a store category page, so people are in shopping mode. A golf blog cannot match that intent, so it should skip the keyword.

## What to do with a mixed SERP

A mixed SERP is a results page that shows more than one type of content. The Ahrefs course uses "golf bags" as an example. The top result is a category page. Next come blog posts about the best golf bags, one guide on how to buy them and more category pages.

When you see a mixed SERP, follow these steps:

1. **Find the dominant type.** Count which content type appears most, especially in the top 3.
2. **Check what you can build.** A store can build a category page. A blog can build a list post. Do not force a page type you cannot do well.
3. **Look for a gap.** Top pages that do not closely match intent are a weakness. For "best convertible car seats for small cars", the Ahrefs course found some top pages were broad car seat lists. A page focused on small cars has a real chance there.
4. **Try a narrower keyword.** If the main term is too mixed, a more specific version often has a clear intent.

## How search intent affects AI Overviews

Informational keywords are where AI answers show up most. In Ahrefs data from its AEO course, AI Overviews appear on about 21% of all keywords and nearly 58% of question queries. And 99.9% of keywords that trigger AI Overviews are informational. Ahrefs also found that an AI Overview reduces the click-through rate for the number one result by 58%.

So intent now shapes your goal, not just your page. Commercial and transactional keywords still send clicks. For informational keywords, the aim often shifts to getting your brand cited in the AI answer. Learn how in [our guide to answer engine optimization](/learn/what-is-aeo).

## How to check that your page matches intent

You can check any page against search intent in a few minutes:

- Google your target keyword and note the 3 C's of the top results.
- Compare your page's type, format and angle with what ranks.

## FAQ

### What are the 4 types of search intent?

The four types are informational, commercial, transactional and navigational. Informational searchers want to learn. Commercial searchers compare options before they buy. Transactional searchers are ready to buy, and navigational searchers want a specific brand's website.

### What are the 3 C's of search intent?

The 3 C's are content type, content format and content angle. Type is the kind of page, such as a blog post or product page. Format is the layout, such as a list or how-to. Angle is the hook, such as "for beginners" or the current year.

### How do I find the search intent of a keyword?

Google the keyword and look at the top 10 results. The kind of pages that rank shows what Google thinks searchers want. Match the type, format and angle that most top pages share.

### Can I rank if my page does not match search intent?

It is very unlikely. Google ranks the pages that best satisfy the searcher's goal. A product page will not rank where every top result is a how-to guide, however strong your site is.
